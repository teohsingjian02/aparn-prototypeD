APARN website redesign

Four prototypes for discussion

Four directions for a new website for the Asia Pacific Artistic Research Network (APARN), prepared for Dr Roopesh Sitharan, Director of APARN. All four use the same checked content and the same functions, so they can be compared fairly. They differ in look, tone and the way you move through them.

These are proposal demos, not the official APARN website. aparn.net is unchanged. Search engines are asked not to index these pages.

Another concept

Concept A: Editorial Gallery

Calm, warm and editorial: large serif headlines on light pages, made for easy reading.

Open Concept A
Art direction
Warm, light pages with soft gradient light in the background, Newsreader for reading and Work Sans for controls, a cinnabar-to-amber gradient for actions, pill buttons and very few boxes. Original “isles” plates mark each conference year.
How it works
A short, guided home page: one headline, the network in figures, why join, how to take part, and a closing invitation. The headline rises in word by word and the artwork unveils; sections drift in as you scroll. The network explorer, a tabbed index beside a reading panel, lives on the Network page, and the archive is a horizontal timeline.

Another concept

Concept B: Living Network

Bold, luminous and energetic: a network glowing in the dark.

Open Concept B
Art direction
Dark pages with jade, cyan, amber and red gradients, heavy condensed poster type (Archivo), glowing network lines, gradient poster cards for every gathering, and pill buttons.
How it works
The same calm home page as the others, with a poster headline whose gradient glides across. The connection board on the Network page sets people, places, activities and practices side by side: pick one and its links draw themselves. A slim bar keeps Explore, Take part, Publish and Join APARN in reach, with a full-screen index.

Concept C: Clear Signal

Clean, precise and technological: the most technological of the four.

Open Concept C
Art direction
White pages with a blue-to-violet gradient, Roboto in a strict scale, rounded buttons and cards, and an original two-line transit diagram of every gathering since 2019 that draws itself.

The same calm home page, with a section index and back-to-top. The headline rises in, figures count up and sections glide in from the direction you scroll. The network explorer on the Network page is an orbit: choose anything and its connections arrange around it.

Concept D: Monochrome

Classic, low-key and strong: black title areas and a strict black and grey hierarchy, like the opening of a film.

Open Concept D
Art direction
Black title areas over calm white reading pages, Manrope (the typeface of aparn.net) in a strict black and grey hierarchy, hairlines instead of boxes, black and white pill buttons, a few words in polished silver, and an original dial that marks every APARN Conference since 2019.
How it works
The home page opens like a film: the APARN mark appears on black with a sweep of light, then the curtain lifts on one large title and a dial whose hand sweeps past every meeting, lighting each one as it passes. Below it, the same calm home page as the others, with a section index. Light follows the pointer across the black title areas, a hairline draws across above every section and rows turn black as you point at them. The Network page has a tabbed explorer beside a reading panel, and the archive is a register of every gathering with filters.

Strengths and trade-offs

Concept A: Editorial Gallery

Strengths

  • The easiest to read, with large type and calm spacing.
  • Feels trustworthy to researchers, institutions and partners.
  • Gives artworks and writing the most room.
  • Very clear on phones.

Trade-offs

  • The quietest of the four: activity is told more than shown.
  • Depends on strong images and good writing to stay lively.

Concept B: Living Network

Strengths

  • The most striking and memorable of the three.
  • Shows the network’s reach and connections vividly.
  • Strong, repeated invitations to join and take part.

Trade-offs

  • Dark pages suit screens better than print.
  • The board is dense on small phones, where it becomes stacked lists with the story first.

Concept C: Clear Signal

Strengths

  • The clearest hierarchy and the quickest to scan.
  • Feels current and technological while staying calm.
  • Motion explains relationships (the orbit, the transit lines).

Trade-offs

  • Less artistic character than A or B; the diagrams carry the personality.
  • White, rounded interfaces are common; the gradient and diagrams keep it distinct.

Concept D: Monochrome

Strengths

  • The most serious and confident of the four: it feels important from the first second.
  • Closest to the current aparn.net (Manrope, black and grey), so it reads as a step forward rather than a break.
  • A very clear hierarchy: black for what matters, grey for what supports it.
  • Photographs and artworks stand out against the restrained palette.

Trade-offs

  • The opening titles take about two seconds on a first visit (any key, click or scroll skips them).
  • Without colour, warmth has to come from the photographs and the wording.

What all four share

  • “Join APARN” opens the APARN Google Group. It sits in the header, the phone menu, the opening screen, after the network section and in a closing invitation, with a note that joining happens on Google Groups.
  • Across the network: dated evidence that keeps members, conference participation, host locations and project locations apart.
  • Three ways to take part, each with a status label: the APARN Conference (next: APARN2027 at RMIT University Vietnam, details to be confirmed), the APARN Gathering at the SAR Conference (proposed 5–10-minute updates; SAR 2027 runs 21–23 April 2027 in Bratislava) and the APARN Annual Publication (proposed).
  • One template, one contribution, two uses, with the “Template for Annual Publication” button on the home page, the publication page, the SAR gathering page and the start of the submission journey.
  • The template as an editable DOCX, a PDF reading copy and a web preview, with a presentation guide built on the same structure.
  • A submission demo in four steps: download, prepare, add details, review. It asks only for what is needed, the publication checkbox starts unchecked, and nothing is uploaded, sent or stored.
  • An archive of every gathering since 2019, with programmes, organisers and links to the published work.
  • Content kept in JSON files, pages readable without JavaScript, keyboard and screen-reader support, and layouts checked at 360, 768 and 1440 pixels.
  • Smooth motion in all four: links scroll smoothly up and down, sections glide in from the direction you scroll, figures count up, headlines rise in and their gradient words keep flowing, buttons respond to a press and pages fade into each other.

Proposal summary for Dr Roopesh Sitharan, Director of APARN

The current aparn.net is accurate but static and text-heavy. All four prototypes keep every verified fact and add what the site is missing: a clear way in, visible activity across the region, simple navigation, artistic character and one easy path from a short SAR update to the year-end publication.

  1. Joining is always one step away. “Join APARN” is the strongest button on every page and goes straight to the Google Group. A line beside it says that joining happens on Google Groups, and five benefits explain why to join, each backed by a dated source.
  2. International activity you can see. Across the network brings together people, places, events and published work: 11 documented places plus the online editions, eight annual meetings since 2019 and 28 pieces in APARN’s JAR channel. Figures are dated and never mix conference participation with membership. Member locations stay private until APARN approves data to show.
  3. Navigation that stays simple. Concepts A, C and D use five sections and Concept B three, in place of a long list of event pages. Every past event has its own archive page, and every address works as a direct link.
  4. Artistic character. Credited artworks from APARN’s own events, original graphics for each concept and warm, direct wording. The four directions offer a calm editorial voice, an energetic exhibition voice, a clear, technological one or a classic, serious one.
  5. From SAR update to publication. One template, one contribution, two uses: prepare one contribution, share a 5–10-minute update at the APARN Gathering at the SAR Conference, and opt in to the year-end collection. The demo shows the whole journey honestly, and the README sets out what a live submission service would need.

Please choose a direction (or a mix), confirm the open items below, and approve the draft template limits. With those answers the chosen concept can be prepared for launch.

  7. Rebordering the Archipelago: the JAR channel dates it 2024, the January 2026 newsletter 2025 (it was launched in Porto in 2025).
